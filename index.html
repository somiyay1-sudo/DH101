Physics Research

Trapping atomic ions for quantum networks.

Trapping Atomic Ions for Quantum Network

Yugen Somiya

Read physics research

What is "Ion Trapping", Why it's important?

Trapping atomic ions is an approach to quantum networks that provides excellent isolation and enables stable quantum states to be maintained for extended periods. By confining ions within an electrode, it becomes possible to precisely manipulate and generate photons using lasers. This approach to quantum information improves the security and speed of communication in our daily lives by using photons to connect quantum memories.

My Research, Photoionization of Lanthanum

Doubly ionized lanthanum (La2+) is proposed as a candidate for quantum networks because La2+ is unique as an ion that can be laser cooled and directly produce infrared photons. These infrared photons should be able to be transmitted through optical fiber with very low loss. A necessary prerequisite for these applications is to produce and trap the ions. In this project, we will create double-ionized lanthanum by photoionization. Photoionization is the process by which atoms are ionized when they absorb a photon with sufficient energy to eject their electrons, thereby transforming the neutral particle into a positive ion. In our research, it will take three steps to remove an electron from a single ionized lanthanum atom that is initially in the ground state. The photoionization is of doubly ionized lanthanum done by a two-photon process. At first, the laser light with 706 nm, resonated with the optical transition driving the transition between the ground state and the metastable state of 3F2o. Then, another laser with 258 nm to excite from the metastable state to another excited state of 3G3.

Maximize contributions to science

I will dedicate my career to physics, particularly the study of atomic structure and optics, as well as exploring their applications in quantum information. This is the best way for me to contribute to the field of science. I believe that continuing my research in this field is the most direct path to achieving meaningful results and the best way to return the favor to Denison. In junior and senior year I will focus on quantum physics and optics as well as the continued research on trapping ions. After graduating from Denison, I would like to attend a Ph.D. program to refine my experimental skills by actively testing and iterating ideas in a hands-on laboratory environment.

Reference: Olmschenk, S. "Doubly Ionized Lanthanum as a Qubit Candidate for Quantum Networks." Physical Review A 105(4), 2022.
