Research Notes (300 words)

For this project, I conducted an analysis drawing upon resources from Amnesty International, Save the Children, Human Rights Watch, and the U.S. Department of Labor. During the research phase, I utilized tools such as ChatGPT and Perplexity to summarize the contents of past reports issued by human rights organizations. While it was relatively easy to grasp the general overview of mineral extraction in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, including issues like child labor and substandard working conditions, finding specific references to actual accidents proved extremely difficult due to a scarcity of detailed information. Ultimately, I discovered a report on Save the Children's website describing an incident in which a child was killed in a landslide while working. This experience highlighted how specific details regarding such shocking and sensitive accidents are often hard to locate, almost as if they are being deliberately concealed. Through this project, I was astonished to discover that the wage gap between the boys working in the DR Congo and the employees of U.S. tech companies is even wider than I had imagined. Of course, I acknowledge that the cost of living differs significantly between the Congo and the United States; furthermore, given that Apple invented the iPhone, it is understandable that profits would flow to companies of this nature. However, I found it utterly shocking that, despite these corporations generating colossal profits, labor practices reminiscent of the colonial era continue to persist in the region today. While some attempt to justify this low-wage labor by arguing that it enables consumers in the U.S. to purchase electronic devices at lower prices, I believe such reasoning is fundamentally flawed. The profits derived from the labor of Congolese boys, who are effectively treated as slaves, are utilized neither for the benefit of consumers nor for the Congolese people themselves, but rather to serve the vested interests of a select few within the tech industry.

Design Statement (250 words)

I chose to create an infographic to convey information and data in a visually accessible manner. The infographic format allowed me to consolidate all relevant details onto a single sheet; by utilizing elements such as graphs, icons, and tables, I was able to present the specific narrative I wished to share in an objective and explanatory way. In particular, by guiding the viewer's gaze from top to bottom and arranging the text in a logical sequence, I was able to communicate the information to the audience with maximum efficiency. Furthermore, as someone with a background in the sciences, I found the simple, explanatory structure of an infographic much easier to work with than attempting to express myself through the more artistic and stylistic mediums of zines or complex layouts. I envisioned my friends as the primary audience for this infographic. Given that they likely have little prior interest in the "invisible workers" within the AI industry, I began with the very first section at the top, emphasizing the harsh realities and significant impact of future mineral resource extraction. Additionally, I kept the explanations simple enough to be understood without any specialized background knowledge, and I selected highly reputable sources, such as Human Rights Watch and the U.S. government, to back up my claims. My hope is that this infographic will prompt my friends to reflect on the "invisible workers" and the dominant social structures surrounding them that exist behind the AI and electronic devices they use in their daily lives. That said, I do not expect my friends to read this infographic and feel compelled to criticize the industry or adopt any specific emotional stance; I believe they should be free to form their own independent impressions. Through this infographic, my sole intention is to provide a "catalyst", a starting point for thinking about the workers who typically remain "invisible."
