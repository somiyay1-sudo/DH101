# Will AI Destroy the Environment?

## Research Documentation (300 words)

Information regarding water usage at Google's data center in Ohio was cited from data published by Penn State Extension. Additionally, data on the facility's water consumption was drawn from scientific papers referenced in the citations. These sources provided crucial insights into why data centers consume such vast quantities of water and the measures companies are implementing to address this issue. Furthermore, similar materials suggested that data centers face not only water-related issues but also problems concerning noise and massive power consumption. However, in many instances, detailed figures regarding data center water usage were not publicly disclosed. This was particularly true for facilities in the Arizona region; perhaps due to their location in an arid environment--where they compete with residential sectors for water resources--virtually no information on their water consumption could be found. Furthermore, even when direct water usage figures were made public, it was even more difficult to uncover data regarding the "embedded" water consumption associated with electricity generation--specifically, the water used to power the data centers themselves. In many cases, local governments and corporations have entered into non-disclosure agreements regarding such matters, making it highly probable that this information is being intentionally concealed. These arrangements appear to have been established for political reasons--specifically, to incentivize tech companies to locate their facilities in these regions. Consequently, there remain many uncertainties surrounding data center water usage. We also encountered instances where local residents--who are directly impacted by these operations--were not informed about the actual volume of water being consumed or the extent to which that water was being appropriately recycled. If local residents had been fully informed about the environmental issues caused by data centers, they might not have approved their construction. Studies suggest that politicians actively court tech companies, which, driven by profit, construct data centers and conceal their environmental impact.

## Artist Statement (250 words)

My primary concern regarding data centers is water scarcity. Despite contributing little to local economies, data centers consume enormous quantities of water. For example, Intel factories consume vast amounts of water, but in return, they generate local employment. Data centers, however, consume enormous amounts of electricity and water yet fail to increase local employment. While this may not pose a significant issue in water-rich regions like Ohio, it becomes a major problem in arid zones such as Arizona.
The proliferation of data centers implies a corresponding reduction in the amount of water available for our use. The amount of water available is limited in each region, and the massive water consumption by data centers has the potential to restrict water usage for local households. In this respect, AI is not sustainable. Furthermore, it is widely predicted that as AI advances, human employment opportunities will diminish. If AI simultaneously consumes vast amounts of water and displaces our jobs, humanity will suffer from both joblessness and water shortages. For AI to be inherently sustainable, its expansion must be pursued in a manner that benefits not only the profits of tech companies but also the environment.
At this point, the question remains: will AI benefits outweigh environmental destruction? I do not believe so. I contend that the unchecked advancement of AI harbors the potential for environmental devastation that lies beyond human control. In other words, if AI is allowed to develop haphazardly--leading to an excessive proliferation of data centers--it could degrade the environment; far from enriching human lives, it risks making them worse. To prevent this outcome, governments and corporations must exercise appropriate oversight; however, our own consumer behavior is equally critical. As consumers, we must recognize the environmental risks associated with AI usage, and it is equally important that we refrain from using AI wastefully. The Japanese language possesses a concept known as *mottainai*. This term encapsulates a sense of concern regarding the use of resources beyond what is truly necessary. Using AI more than is required constitutes *mottainai* behavior; it is our collective responsibility--as consumers--to recognize that such actions amount to the wasteful depletion of environmental resources.
